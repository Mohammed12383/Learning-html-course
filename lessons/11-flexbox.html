Layout with Flexbox

Up to now, boxes stacked on top of each other. Real websites put things side by side: menus, rows of cards, a picture next to text. Flexbox is the tool for that, and it's easier than it sounds.

  • Side by side with one line
  • Container and items
  • Spacing things out
  • Centering anything
  • Navigation bars and card rows

One line puts things side by side

Block boxes stack on top of each other. Add display: flex; to the box around them, and they line up in a row.

Container and items

Flexbox always has two parts:

  • The container: the parent box. You put display: flex on this one.
  • The items: the boxes directly inside it. They get arranged.

Think of the container as a shelf, and the items as books on it. You tell the shelf how to arrange the books.

main axis (the direction of the row) → cross axis
123

Flexbox thinks in two directions. The main axis is the direction items line up (left to right in a row). The cross axis goes the other way (top to bottom).

The flexbox playground

These five properties go on the container. Change each one and watch the boxes move.

PropertyWhat it does
flex-directionrow (side by side, the default) or column (stacked).
justify-contentSpreads items along the main axis: at the start, center, end, or with space between.
align-itemsLines items up on the cross axis: top, center, bottom, or stretched to fill.
gapSpace between items. Much easier than margins.
flex-wrapwrap lets items move to a new line when there isn't enough room.

How to remember justify vs align

justify-content = along the row (the main direction). align-items = up and down (the other direction). When you switch to column, they swap: justify becomes up and down.

Center anything

For years, centering things in the middle of a box was famously hard in CSS. With flexbox, it's three lines. This is one of the most useful tricks you'll ever learn:

Growing to fill space

The properties so far go on the container. One very useful property goes on an item: flex: 1. It means "grow and take up the leftover space".

The button keeps its size, and the text box stretches to fill the rest. If two items both have flex: 1, they share the space equally. flex: 2 takes twice as much as flex: 1.

Common mistakes

  • Putting display: flex on the items instead of the container around them.
  • Expecting it to work on grandchildren. Flexbox only arranges the direct children. For boxes deeper inside, make another flex container (like the links in the nav bar).
  • Mixing up justify-content and align-items. Just try the other one!

Where do you put display: flex?

Flex goes on the container (the parent). It arranges the items directly inside it.

Which justify-content value puts the logo on the far left and the menu on the far right?

space-between puts all the free space between the items, pushing the first and last ones to the edges.

Remember

  • display: flex on the container puts its children side by side.
  • justify-content = along the row. align-items = up and down.
  • gap for space between. flex-wrap: wrap to wrap.
  • Center anything: display: flex; justify-content: center; align-items: center;
  • flex: 1 on an item makes it fill the leftover space.
