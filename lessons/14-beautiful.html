Make It Beautiful

Modern app websites feel soft, colorful and alive. Things float, glow and wiggle a little when you touch them. None of that is magic. It's a handful of CSS tricks, and this lesson teaches all of them.

  • Breathing room

The recipe for a fun, polished website

Look closely at a beautiful app website and you'll find the same ingredients again and again:

space

Lots of empty space around everything. Nothing feels crowded.

soft color

Pastel backgrounds, one or two bright colors, dark gray text.

round

Rounded corners on cards, buttons and pictures.

depth

Soft shadows and gradients that make things float.

motion

Small, smooth animations when you hover, click or scroll.

big type

Large, bold, friendly headings with short text.

3. Gradients

A gradient blends from one color into another. It's written as a background. linear-gradient blends in a straight line, at an angle you choose:

deg means degrees, like a compass: 0deg goes bottom to top, 90deg goes left to right, 180deg goes top to bottom.

More gradient tricks

The "soft blobs" background is very popular on modern landing pages. It's just a few round gradients that fade to transparent, stacked on top of each other with commas.

4. Things that react: hover effects

You learned :hover and transition with buttons. They work on anything. Combined with transform, which moves, grows or turns an element, you get lovely effects:

transformWhat it does
translateY(-6px)Moves up 6px (a "lift"). translateX moves sideways.
scale(1.05)Grows to 105% size. scale(0.95) shrinks.
rotate(-3deg)Turns a little. Negative = counter-clockwise.

Look at .spin:hover .emoji: it means "the emoji inside a card that's being hovered". So hovering the card animates something inside it.

5. Animations that play by themselves

A transition needs a change (like hover) to start. An animation plays on its own, and can loop forever. You make one in two steps:

  1. Describe the movement with @keyframes and a name you choose. from is the start, to is the end, or use percentages for more steps.
  2. Play it on an element with the animation property: the name, how long, and how.
animation: property
float keyframes name
3s how long
ease-in-out speed: slow-fast-slow
infiniteloop forever

Design checklist

Before you call a page done, check these. They're what separates "made by a beginner" from "made by a designer":

  • Two fonts at most. One for headings, one for text.
  • One main color, plus soft versions of it and neutrals (white, grays, dark text).
  • The same corner rounding on similar things (for example, 16px on all cards, 999px on all buttons).
  • Spacing from your scale: 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64. No random numbers.
  • Easy to read: dark text on light backgrounds, at least 16px.
  • One main button per section, so people know what to do.
  • Lots of space. Then a little more.
  • Gentle motion: hover effects everywhere you can click, and at most one or two things that move on their own.

You want a box to float up and down forever. What do you need?

Transitions only play when something changes (like hover). @keyframes + animation play by themselves, and infinite makes them loop.

How do you use a CSS variable called --main?

Create it with --main: #FF5C8A; and use it with var(--main).

Remember

  • More space makes everything look better.
  • Variables: :root { --main: #FF5C8A; } then var(--main).
  • linear-gradient(135deg, color1, color2) and radial-gradient(...).
  • transform (translate, scale, rotate) + transition for hover effects.
  • @keyframes name { ... } + animation: name 2s ease infinite;
  • backdrop-filter: blur(16px) for glass.
