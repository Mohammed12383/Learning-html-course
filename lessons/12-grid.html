Layout with Grid

Flexbox arranges things in one line. Grid arranges things in rows and columns at the same time, like a table or a chessboard. It's perfect for photo galleries, card layouts and whole pages.

Your first grid

Put display: grid on the container, then say how many columns you want with grid-template-columns. The items fill the columns in order, left to right, and start new rows by themselves.

repeat(3, 1fr) is a short way to write 1fr 1fr 1fr, which means three equal columns.

What is fr?

fr means fraction: a share of the free space. 1fr 1fr 1fr is three equal shares. 2fr 1fr makes the first column twice as wide as the second. You can mix it with pixels too.

Turn on X-ray and compare the widths of each column. With 200px 1fr, the first column is exactly 200px and the second takes everything else. That's a classic "sidebar + content" layout.

A grid that fits every screen

This one line is the most famous grid trick. It makes as many columns as will fit, each at least 160px wide, and stretches them to fill the row. On a phone you get 2 columns, on a laptop 6 or more, with no extra code.

  • repeat(auto-fit, ...): "make as many columns as fit".
  • minmax(160px, 1fr): "each column is at least 160px, and at most an equal share".

Press Phone, Tablet and Laptop under the preview and watch the columns change by themselves.

Big tiles: spanning

An item can stretch across several columns or rows. On the item, write grid-column: span 2 to take two columns, or grid-row: span 2 to take two rows. This makes the "bento box" layouts that modern app websites love.

Turn on X-ray: every tile lines up with the same invisible columns, even though they're different sizes. That's what makes grid layouts look so tidy.

A whole page layout

Grid can also lay out a whole page. grid-template-areas lets you draw the layout with words:

Each word in grid-template-areas is a cell. Repeating a word ("header header") makes that area cover both columns. Then each element says which area it goes in with grid-area.

Flexbox or grid?

flex

One direction. A row or a column. Best for small groups: a menu, buttons in a row, an icon next to text, centering something.

grid

Two directions. Rows and columns. Best for card layouts, galleries, dashboards and whole pages.

Real websites use both together: grid for the big layout, flexbox for the small pieces inside. There's no wrong answer. If one feels hard, try the other.

Common mistakes

  • Writing grid-template-column without the s. It's grid-template-columns.
  • Using margins between grid items. Use gap instead.
  • Spanning more columns than exist, which makes extra columns appear.

What does grid-template-columns: 1fr 1fr 1fr; make?

Each 1fr is one equal share of the space, so three of them make three equal columns.

How do you make a tile two columns wide?

span 2 stretches the item across two columns. Use grid-row: span 2 for two rows.

Remember

  • display: grid + grid-template-columns on the container.
  • fr = a share of the free space. repeat(3, 1fr) = 3 equal columns.
  • repeat(auto-fit, minmax(160px, 1fr)) fits any screen.
  • grid-column: span 2 makes a big tile. gap for spacing.
